Two weeks ago, a Keith Schnakenberg skeet landed on my feed. He was spreading
the word about a paper he co-authored with Zion Little and Jacob Montgomery on
[pre-registration as cheap talk](https://osf.io/preprints/socarxiv/v7cgj_v1).
My mind flooded with dopamine as metascience and formal theory are two of my
pet interests. After that subsided, though, I looked inward and found
perplexion.

It reminded me that there \*is\* this alien, neuronormative social realm out
there, because 'cheap talk' is an oxymoron to me. My specific flavour of
neurodivergence makes it such that I incur mental anguish when I flake. I make
promises in order to bind myself to a commitment, for crying out loud.

For the same reason, I’ve also become somewhat of a blind advocate of
pre-registration: I only have my perspective, and I genuinely feel like my hands
are cuffed once I’ve submitted a pre-analysis plan. This paper certainly tempers
my zeal (you could think of it as Bayesian updating, haha).

Of course, it may well be the case that I simply haven't been exposed to the
'cutthroat world of academic publishing', and I concede my inexperience.
Nonetheless, as things stand, that vast gulf between me and this exotic realm of
cheap talk still exists.

Or... this may well be the start of a research puzzle. Thinking through this, I
feel like you could model my reluctance to engage in cheap talk as some form of
'cost of lying' $k > 0$, albeit one that is self-inflicted. The term
'endogenously costly signalling' is lingering in my head as a potential
descriptor.

Thinking more substantively, I made a connection in my head between this and my
reluctance to deal with 'high politics' or anything public-facing. I now carry
thoughts in my head about what insights modelling entry into the political arena
may reveal. What unintuitive conclusions could such a model bring forth? I'm
reading articles about neurodivergent politicians and their motivations to enter
politics right now, and it seems promising.

Another idea: disclosure. For politicians already office or some other
public-facing position, how do we model self-disclosure as  as a dynamic game?
It's one of the few instances in which I can recognise backward induction as a
real cognitive process in my own brain. Getting the empirics for this may
require qualitative research (interviews), but it doesn't sound half-bad when
it's with other neurodivergent folks.

Coming back to my thoughts on meta-science, I wonder what the implications of
this are... That we must re-orient the incentive structure such that norms about
truth-telling and lying a.) become more salient, and more importantly, b.)
prevail? Or⸺as pointed out in the article itself⸺we can just embrace
pre-registered reports. I'm also still thinking about that Cyrus Samii tweet on
[presenting your pre-registrations](https://bsky.app/profile/cdsamii.bsky.social/post/3mvo7iba7722m),
and I'm on board with it.

There's still much to flesh out and explore. Time will tell see where all this
takes me.
